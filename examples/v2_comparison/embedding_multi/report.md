## Retrieval-Augmented Generation

A prominent line of research addresses the factual limitations of large language models by grounding generation in externally retrieved documents. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) frameworks supplement model parameters with dynamic access to knowledge corpora, reducing reliance on potentially outdated or incorrect memorized information. However, naive retrieval does not guarantee that retrieved documents are relevant or reliable, motivating more sophisticated approaches.

Self-RAG trains a single language model to adaptively decide *when* retrieval is necessary and to critically evaluate the quality of both retrieved passages and its own generated outputs through special "reflection tokens" [53dea3c2]. This on-demand retrieval strategy allows the model to avoid unnecessary retrieval for straightforward queries while invoking retrieval selectively for knowledge-intensive tasks. Self-RAG significantly outperforms ChatGPT and retrieval-augmented Llama 2-chat on open-domain question answering, reasoning, and fact verification benchmarks, and improves factuality and citation accuracy for long-form generation [53dea3c2].

Building on the observation that retrieved documents vary widely in quality, Corrective RAG (CRAG) introduces a lightweight retrieval evaluator that assesses document relevance and triggers different downstream actions — including large-scale web searches when the static corpus returns suboptimal results [0e2f3be9]. A decompose-then-recompose algorithm further filters irrelevant content from retrieved documents before passing information to the generator. CRAG is designed as a plug-and-play module compatible with existing RAG pipelines, including Self-RAG, and demonstrates consistent improvements across four datasets spanning both short- and long-form generation [0e2f3be9].

R2AG addresses a complementary challenge: the semantic gap between retrieval systems and the language models they serve. By introducing an R2-Former architecture to capture nuanced retrieval features and a retrieval-aware prompting strategy, R2AG incorporates retrieval metadata directly into the generation process [7f782037]. Experiments across five datasets confirm that retrieval information acts as an "anchor" that guides generation and mitigates hallucination, with the system supporting low-resource scenarios in which both retrievers and LLMs remain frozen [7f782037]. Together, these works establish that reliable RAG requires not only retrieving documents but carefully evaluating, filtering, and communicating retrieval signals to the generator.

## Fine-Tuning with Human Feedback

While retrieval targets factual grounding at inference time, a separate cluster of research focuses on shaping model behavior through post-training supervision signals derived from human preferences. InstructGPT demonstrates that fine-tuning a language model with human feedback — first via supervised learning on expert demonstrations and then via reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) on ranked model outputs — dramatically improves instruction-following behavior [3f8c0e98]. Strikingly, a 1.3B-parameter InstructGPT model fine-tuned in this manner is preferred by human evaluators over the raw 175B GPT-3 model, illustrating that alignment quality can outweigh raw scale [3f8c0e98]. The fine-tuned models also show improvements in truthfulness and reductions in toxic output generation, with minimal regression on standard NLP benchmarks [3f8c0e98].

Llama 2 extends this paradigm to open-source models, releasing a family of pretrained models (7B–70B parameters) alongside Llama 2-Chat variants fine-tuned through supervised fine-tuning and RLHF [c8ef7ce3]. The fine-tuning pipeline incorporates over one million human-annotated examples and is complemented by comprehensive safety measures including pretraining data filtering, safety-focused fine-tuning, and extensive red teaming [c8ef7ce3]. Human evaluations suggest Llama 2-Chat models outperform existing open-source chat models and may serve as viable alternatives to proprietary closed-source systems [c8ef7ce3]. By detailing their fine-tuning methodology, the Llama 2 authors also provide a foundation for community-driven alignment research.

## AI-Driven Alignment

A third approach reduces dependence on costly human feedback by leveraging the model itself to generate alignment signals. Constitutional AI (CAI) trains harmless AI assistants using a set of written principles — a "constitution" — rather than relying on extensive human labeling of harmful content [c831e729]. The method proceeds in two stages: first, the model is prompted to critique and revise its own outputs according to the constitutional principles (supervised learning from self-revision); second, AI-generated preference judgments replace human annotators in an RLHF-style reinforcement learning phase [c831e729]. CAI models are designed to be non-evasive, engaging with potentially harmful queries by articulating objections rather than simply refusing, with chain-of-thought reasoning improving the transparency and quality of these evaluations [c831e729]. This approach marks an important step toward scalable oversight, where human effort is concentrated on specifying principles rather than labeling individual outputs.

## Open Questions

Several important gaps and tensions remain across this literature. First, retrieval-based and fine-tuning-based approaches have largely been developed independently; how best to combine adaptive retrieval (e.g., Self-RAG) with RLHF-aligned models remains an open question. Second, while CRAG and R2AG improve retrieval quality, both rely on heuristic evaluators whose failure modes under adversarial or out-of-distribution queries are not fully characterized. Third, Constitutional AI reduces human labeling costs but assumes that a fixed constitution can adequately cover the space of harmful behaviors — how to construct, validate, and update such constitutions is underexplored. Fourth, the relationship between scale and alignment efficiency (e.g., the finding that a 1.3B InstructGPT model outperforms a 175B base model [3f8c0e98]) raises questions about the minimum model capacity needed to benefit from each alignment technique. Finally, most evaluations rely on human preference judgments or benchmark accuracy, leaving open how well these proxy metrics translate to trustworthiness in high-stakes real-world deployments.
