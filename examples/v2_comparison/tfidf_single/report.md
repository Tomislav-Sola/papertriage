## Retrieval-Augmented Generation

A prominent line of research addresses the factuality limitations of large language models (LLMs) by grounding generation in externally retrieved documents. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems supplement a model's parametric knowledge with dynamically retrieved evidence, but naive retrieval pipelines can introduce noise when retrieved documents are of poor quality. To address this, Corrective RAG (CRAG) proposes a lightweight retrieval evaluator that assesses document relevance and triggers different retrieval actions accordingly, extending to large-scale web searches when the static corpus yields suboptimal results [0e2f3be9]. CRAG further introduces a decompose-then-recompose algorithm to filter irrelevant content from retrieved passages before generation, and demonstrates plug-and-play compatibility with existing RAG frameworks such as Self-RAG [0e2f3be9].

A complementary challenge is the semantic gap between retrievers and LLMs, which operate in different representation spaces. R2AG addresses this by introducing R2-Former, an architecture that captures nuanced retrieval features, and a retrieval-aware prompting strategy that integrates retrieval signals directly into the generation process [7f782037]. Experiments across five datasets show that retrieval information acts as an anchor that guides LLMs and mitigates hallucination, and the framework supports low-resource scenarios with frozen retrievers and LLMs [7f782037].

Taking a more deeply integrated approach, Self-RAG trains a single language model to adaptively decide *when* to retrieve and *how* to evaluate its own outputs, using special reflection tokens for both retrieval decisions and critique of generated content [53dea3c2]. This allows controllable inference-time behavior tailored to diverse task requirements. Self-RAG outperforms ChatGPT and retrieval-augmented Llama 2-Chat on open-domain QA, reasoning, and fact verification, while also improving factuality and citation accuracy in long-form generation [53dea3c2]. Together, these works establish that reliable retrieval requires not just access to external knowledge, but intelligent selection, filtering, and integration of that knowledge into the generation process.

## Fine-Tuning with Human Feedback

Beyond retrieval, alignment with human intent and factual reliability can be substantially improved through post-pretraining fine-tuning. InstructGPT demonstrates this by combining supervised fine-tuning on human demonstrations with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), where labelers rank model outputs and these preferences are used to train a reward model [3f8c0e98]. Strikingly, a 1.3B-parameter InstructGPT model trained this way is preferred by human evaluators over the 175B GPT-3 model on the API prompt distribution, illustrating that alignment quality can compensate for raw scale [3f8c0e98]. The approach also reduces toxic output generation and improves truthfulness, with minimal regression on standard NLP benchmarks [3f8c0e98].

Llama 2-Chat extends this paradigm at open-source scale, training models ranging from 7B to 70B parameters using supervised fine-tuning on over one million human-annotated examples followed by iterative RLHF [c8ef7ce3]. Safety is addressed through multiple complementary mechanisms: pretraining data filtering, safety-focused fine-tuning, and structured red-teaming exercises. The resulting models outperform other publicly available chat models on most benchmarks tested and are reported to approach the quality of closed-source systems in human evaluations for helpfulness and safety [c8ef7ce3]. These results confirm that human feedback-driven fine-tuning is a scalable and broadly applicable technique for improving alignment across model families.

## AI-Driven Alignment Feedback

A significant practical limitation of RLHF is its reliance on large quantities of human-labeled preference data. Constitutional AI (CAI) proposes an alternative in which a model is guided by a set of written principles—a "constitution"—and trained through a two-stage process: first, supervised learning using self-critique and revision, and second, reinforcement learning from AI-generated feedback (RLAIF) rather than human annotations [c831e729]. This approach enables the model to evaluate its own outputs for harmlessness and revise them accordingly. CAI further incorporates chain-of-thought reasoning to improve transparency in the model's decision-making process [c831e729]. Importantly, the framework produces non-evasive assistants that engage with sensitive queries by articulating principled objections rather than simply refusing, striking a better balance between helpfulness and harmlessness [c831e729].

## Open Questions

Several important gaps and tensions remain across this literature. First, while retrieval augmentation improves factual grounding, it introduces latency, retrieval errors, and potential conflicts between retrieved and parametric knowledge; how to robustly reconcile these sources remains an open problem. Second, RLHF-based approaches depend heavily on the quality and diversity of human annotators, whose preferences may embed cultural biases or inconsistencies; RLAIF mitigates this but raises questions about which values are encoded in the AI feedback model itself. Third, the interaction between retrieval and fine-tuning is underexplored—methods like Self-RAG train retrieval behavior end-to-end, but whether combining CAI-style constitutional training with adaptive retrieval yields additive benefits is unknown. Finally, most evaluations focus on English-language benchmarks; the extent to which these alignment and retrieval techniques generalize to multilingual and low-resource settings represents a meaningful open direction.
